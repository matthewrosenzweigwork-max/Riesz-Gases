14
15* **Setting of Lemma 3.27.**  The lemma sits in the subsection "Evolution estimates at a fixed positive temperature" (L2515),
16  with the gradient mean-field dynamics of the finite-regularity statement Proposition 5.1 (L5771-5791;
17  `HRS.PDE.FiniteRegHyp`, `HRS.PDE.IsMFSolutionHK`, `M = -I`, `ν = β⁻¹`, `HRS.PDE.mfData`), whose density path `ρ^t`
18  converges exponentially to the thermal density; the TeX does not restate these hypotheses (it refers to
19  `\\cref{sec:finite-regularity}`), so they are the hypotheses of the statement.
20* **The evolution dual to the backward generator on `E_r`** is not a library object; it is characterised by duality
21  with the classical adjoint propagator `HRS.propagator` (Definition 3.5): `⟨V^{t,u} z, φ⟩ = ⟨z, U^{u,t} φ⟩` for smooth `φ`
22  (`IsDualEvolution`).  The lemma asserts that a strongly continuous evolution `V` on `E_r = H^{-r}_0` with this duality
23  exists and satisfies the decay and maximal bounds (existence is the content "the evolution dual to
24  `H:eq:backward-generator` is strongly continuous on `E_r`").
25* **Stochastic convolutions and Hilbert martingales** as in `HRS/Uniform/Spec.lean`.

41/-- `V` is the evolution dual to the backward generator `H:eq:backward-generator` (for the dynamics `D` and the density path
42`ρ`) on `E_m = H^{-m}_0`: `⟨V^{t,u} z, φ⟩ = ⟨z, U^{u,t} φ⟩` for `0 ≤ u ≤ t`, `z ∈ E_m` and smooth `φ`, where `U^{u,t} =
43HRS.propagator D ρ u t` is the adjoint propagator of Definition 3.5 and `⟨F, φ⟩ = NegSobolev.eval F φ`. -/
44def IsDualEvolution (D : DynData d) (ρ : ℝ → Torus d → ℝ) (m : ℝ)
45    (V : ℝ → ℝ → NegSobolev d m →L[ℝ] NegSobolev d m) : Prop :=
46  ∀ u t : ℝ, 0 ≤ u → u ≤ t → ∀ (z : NegSobolev d m) (φ : Torus d → ℝ), Torus.IsSmooth φ →
47    NegSobolev.eval (V t u z) φ = NegSobolev.eval z (propagator D ρ u t φ)

49/-- Lemma 3.27 (`M:P:lem:evolution`, "Maximal estimates and decay", TeX M L2531-2546), at a fixed positive temperature
50`β` for the gradient mean-field flow of Proposition 5.1 (`FiniteRegHyp`, `IsMFSolutionHK`, the regularity index `K`
51sufficiently large depending on `r`: "uniform smooth density and potential bounds", L2557-2558; for each real `r`).
52
53(1) For each real `r` the evolution `V^{t,u}` dual to the backward generator is strongly continuous on `E_r` (a strongly
54continuous evolution `Vev` with `IsDualEvolution` exists); (2) on the zero-mass subspace (all of `E_r = H^{-r}_0`)
55`‖V^{t,u}‖_{E_r→E_r} ≤ C_r e^{-c_r(t-u)}`, `0 ≤ u ≤ t` (`P:eq:decay`); (3) for every `T > 0`, every continuous
56`E_r`-valued martingale `M` with covariance density `C_u`, and every window `[a, a+T]`, `a ≥ 0`, the stochastic convolution
57`∫_a^t V^{t,u} dM_u` has a continuous version with
58`E sup_{a≤t≤a+T} ‖∫_a^t V^{t,u} dM_u‖²_{E_r} ≤ C_{r,T} E ∫_a^{a+T} Tr C_u du` (`P:eq:maximal`), with `C_{r,T}` independent of
59`a` (chosen before `a`).  The two integrals are `lintegral`s in `ℝ≥0∞`.

63positive thermal density), and Gaussian noise is a special case of a continuous martingale; neither is restated.

90/-- Lemma 3.31 (`M:lem:stationary-noise`, "Periodic stationary noise on a fixed observation window", TeX M L2758-2769).
91`μ = ρ_β dx` is a smooth positive periodic thermal equilibrium measure (`IsThermalDensity`, smooth, `ρ_β > 0`, `V` smooth).
92`Q` is the actual stationary particle law (`Law X_t = Q` for all `t ≥ 0`) with `N Var_Q[μ_N(f)] ≤ C ‖f‖²_{L²(μ)}` for
93`f ∈ L²(μ)` (`evariance`, in `ℝ≥0∞`) and `‖dm_N/dμ - 1‖_{L²(μ)} ≤ C δ_N`, `m_N` the one-particle marginal of `Q`.
94At every sufficiently large fixed negative Sobolev index `m` (`m ≥ m₀`), the empirical martingale `M` (an `E_m`-valued
95martingale with covariance density `C_N^t = covForm m β⁻¹ (μ_N^t)`, i.e. the empirical martingale of Lemma 3.24 with
96`σ_N = √N`, `q_N = β⁻¹`: the TeX's "`2β⁻¹μ`" covariance) and a Gaussian martingale `Ḡ` with covariance `D_t = covForm m β⁻¹ μ`
97admit one adapted coupling (an enlargement, `Ḡ` independent of the initial σ-field `𝒢`, `M∘π - Ḡ` a Hilbert martingale; the
98conjuncts of `IsAdaptedGaussianCoupling` for the whole half-line `T = ∞`), whose propagated difference
99`Z_t = ∫_0^t V^{t,u} d(M - Ḡ)_u` (`V` the stationary dual evolution on `E_m`, `IsDualEvolution` for the frozen coefficient
100`ρ_β`) has, on every window `[a, a+T]`, `a ≥ 0`, second-mean path norm
101`‖sup_{a≤t≤a+T} ‖Z_t‖_{E_m}‖_{L²} ≤ C_T (N^{-1/2} + δ_N)`, uniformly in `a ≥ 0` (one coupling, one constant).
102The index threshold `m₀` depends on `(d, s, β, V)` only; the constant `C_T` is chosen after `m`, the constant `Cv` of the hypotheses
103(the TeX's generic `C`) and `T`, and before `N`, `δ_N` and everything on the probability space.  The second mean is a `lintegral` in `ℝ≥0∞`.
104
105Representation: only the covariation of `M` and the stationarity/one-body estimates of the particle law are used (as in the
106TeX proof, L2776-2799), so the particle process is the pair `(X, Q)` and `M` is any martingale with this covariation;
107`V` is any evolution with the stationary duality (it exists, strongly continuous and exponentially decaying, by Lemma 3.27 for
108the stationary solution).  AMBIGUITY: the "adjoint gap" (exponential decay of `V^{t,u}`, L2800-2801) is not a hypothesis here
109because it is a consequence of the duality with the (frozen-coefficient) propagator (Lemma 3.27). -/

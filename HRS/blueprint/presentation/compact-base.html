1. The sharp sub-Coulomb commutator

For 0≤𝗌<𝖽−20\le\mathsf{s}<\mathsf{d}-2, the sharp commutator estimate combines the transport regularity ∣∇∣(𝖽−𝗌)/2v∈L2𝖽/(𝖽−𝗌−2)|\nabla|^{(\mathsf{d}-\mathsf{s})/2}v\in L^{2\mathsf{d}/(\mathsf{d}-\mathsf{s}-2)} with the correction ∥ρ∥∞𝗌/𝖽N𝗌/𝖽−1\|\rho\|_\infty^{\mathsf{s}/\mathsf{d}}N^{\mathsf{s}/\mathsf{d}-1}. The whole-space and periodic theorems allow real signed densities of mass one. The periodic specialization gives the sub-Coulomb commutator estimate in HRS.

1.1. Whole-space argument

At unit scale, for bounded compactly supported densities, split the force into high and low frequencies, then split the transport in the low-frequency term. This gives three contributions: high-frequency force, low-frequency force with low-frequency transport, and low-frequency force with high-frequency transport. The last is a particle sum minus a background integral; estimate these separately and add the three bounds.

1.2. Rescaling and density approximation

Rescaling gives the arbitrary-scale estimate. A common density approximation preserves mass and gives convergence of the density norms, energies and commutators. Passing to the limit removes the temporary boundedness and support assumptions. Finally, take p=∞p=\infty and η=λ=(N∥ρ∥∞)−1/𝖽\eta=\lambda=(N\|\rho\|_\infty)^{-1/\mathsf{d}}. The two error terms agree:

λ−𝗌N=∥ρ∥∞λ𝖽−𝗌=∥ρ∥∞𝗌/𝖽N𝗌/𝖽−1.\frac{\lambda^{-\mathsf{s}}}{N}=\|\rho\|_\infty\lambda^{\mathsf{d}-\mathsf{s}}=\|\rho\|_\infty^{\mathsf{s}/\mathsf{d}}N^{\mathsf{s}/\mathsf{d}-1}.(3.1)

The logarithmic renormalization remains −log⁡λ/(2N)-\log\lambda/(2N) when 𝗌=0\mathsf{s}=0.

1.3. Relation to the earlier sharp-error estimate

The sub-Coulomb note of 13 October 2026, Introduction and Theorem 1.1, compares this bound with the earlier estimate of Hess-Childs, Rosenzweig and Serfaty, which required

∥∣∇∣𝖺/2v∥L2𝖽/(𝖺−2),𝖽<𝖺<𝖽+2.\bigl\||\nabla|^{\mathsf{a}/2}v\bigr\|_{L^{2\mathsf{d}/(\mathsf{a}-2)}},\qquad \mathsf{d}<\mathsf{a}<\mathsf{d}+2.(3.2)

The note obtains 𝖺=𝖽−𝗌\mathsf{a}=\mathsf{d}-\mathsf{s} with the same N𝗌/𝖽−1N^{\mathsf{s}/\mathsf{d}-1} error.

1.4. Periodic estimates

The periodic sharp theorem has the same density-dependent bound. Its kernel has singular part ∣x∣−𝗌|x|^{-\mathsf{s}} for 𝗌>0\mathsf{s}>0, whereas the whole-space kernel is 𝗌−1∣x∣−𝗌\mathsf{s}^{-1}|x|^{-\mathsf{s}}; both have logarithmic singularity −log⁡∣x∣-\log|x| at 𝗌=0\mathsf{s}=0.

1.5. The HRS application

For the HRS application, specialize to 0≤ρ≤Λ0\le\rho\le\Lambda, Λ≥1\Lambda\ge1 and N≥2N\ge2. The periodic embedding bounds the sharp transport seminorm by the higher-derivative seminorm in the application:

∥∣∇∣(𝖽−𝗌)/2v∥L2𝖽/(𝖽−𝗌−2)≤C𝖽,𝗌∥∣∇∣(𝖽+1)/2v∥L2𝖽/(𝖽−1).\bigl\||\nabla|^{(\mathsf{d}-\mathsf{s})/2}v\bigr\|_{L^{2\mathsf{d}/(\mathsf{d}-\mathsf{s}-2)}}\le C_{\mathsf{d},\mathsf{s}}\bigl\||\nabla|^{(\mathsf{d}+1)/2}v\bigr\|_{L^{2\mathsf{d}/(\mathsf{d}-1)}}.(3.3)

Bounding ∥ρ∥∞\|\rho\|_\infty by Λ\Lambda gives 𝗈N(Λ)\mathsf{o}_N(\Lambda). Combining this specialization with the Lipschitz estimate for 𝖽−2≤𝗌<𝖽\mathsf{d}-2\le\mathsf{s}<\mathsf{d} yields the all-regime result.

2. Mesoscopic observations and screening

We compare finitely many mesoscopic observations of the periodic Riesz diffusion with Gaussian vectors, uniformly in the observation times. The error determines admissible shrinking scales and yields a stationary limit after a diverging rescaled waiting time.

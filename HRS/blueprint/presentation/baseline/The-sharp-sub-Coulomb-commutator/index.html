1. The sharp sub-Coulomb commutator🔗

For 0≤𝗌<𝖽−20\le\mathsf{s}<\mathsf{d}-20≤s<d−2, the sharp commutator estimate combines the transport regularity ∣∇∣(𝖽−𝗌)/2v∈L2𝖽/(𝖽−𝗌−2)|\nabla|^{(\mathsf{d}-\mathsf{s})/2}v\in L^{2\mathsf{d}/(\mathsf{d}-\mathsf{s}-2)}∣∇∣(d−s)/2v∈L2d/(d−s−2) with the correction ∥ρ∥∞𝗌/𝖽N𝗌/𝖽−1\|\rho\|_\infty^{\mathsf{s}/\mathsf{d}}N^{\mathsf{s}/\mathsf{d}-1}∥ρ∥∞s/d​Ns/d−1. The whole-space and periodic theorems allow real signed densities of mass one. The periodic specialization gives the sub-Coulomb commutator estimate in HRS.

1.1. Whole-space argument🔗

At unit scale, for bounded compactly supported densities, split the force into high and low frequencies, then split the transport in the low-frequency term. This gives three contributions: high-frequency force, low-frequency force with low-frequency transport, and low-frequency force with high-frequency transport. The last is a particle sum minus a background integral; estimate these separately and add the three bounds.

1.2. Rescaling and density approximation🔗

Rescaling gives the arbitrary-scale estimate. A common density approximation preserves mass and gives convergence of the density norms, energies and commutators. Passing to the limit removes the temporary boundedness and support assumptions. Finally, take p=∞p=\inftyp=∞ and η=λ=(N∥ρ∥∞)−1/𝖽\eta=\lambda=(N\|\rho\|_\infty)^{-1/\mathsf{d}}η=λ=(N∥ρ∥∞​)−1/d. The two error terms agree:

1.4. Periodic estimates🔗

The periodic sharp theorem has the same density-dependent bound. Its kernel has singular part ∣x∣−𝗌|x|^{-\mathsf{s}}∣x∣−s for 𝗌>0\mathsf{s}>0s>0, whereas the whole-space kernel is 𝗌−1∣x∣−𝗌\mathsf{s}^{-1}|x|^{-\mathsf{s}}s−1∣x∣−s; both have logarithmic singularity −log⁡∣x∣-\log|x|−log∣x∣ at 𝗌=0\mathsf{s}=0s=0.

1.5. The HRS application🔗

For the HRS application, specialize to 0≤ρ≤Λ0\le\rho\le\Lambda0≤ρ≤Λ, Λ≥1\Lambda\ge1Λ≥1 and N≥2N\ge2N≥2. The periodic embedding bounds the sharp transport seminorm by the higher-derivative seminorm in the application:

§S11 · Relaxation on moving windows

Start a gas at preparation parameter γ\gamma and observe it on [u,u+T][u,u+T]. How large must uu be before the stationary Gaussian law and its first correction describe the fluctuations to order o(N−1/2)o(N^{-1/2})? Proposition S11.1 gives separate rates for the covariance and cubic tensor. Corollary S11.2 compares them with the error at finite NN.

Setting and notation

Fix 𝖽≥2\mathsf d\ge 2, 0≤𝗌<𝖽/20\le\mathsf s<\mathsf d/2, a,β,T>0a,\beta,T>0 and γ≥0\gamma\ge 0, with γ>0\gamma>0 when 𝗌>0\mathsf s>0. The confining potential is V(x)=a∣x∣2/2V(x)=a|x|^2/2. Let μ=ρβ dx\mu=\rho_\beta\,\mathrm dx be a thermal equilibrium with Poincaré constant λ>0\lambda>0, and put κ=λ/β\kappa=\lambda/\beta. The observation space is 𝒳T=C([0,T];B24𝖽+240)\mathcal X_T=C([0,T];B_{24\mathsf d+240}), where BmB_m is the dual of the oscillator space Σm\Sigma^m.

For θ∈{γ,β}\theta\in\{\gamma,\beta\}, take a linear Gaussian evolution about ρβ\rho_\beta on every finite time interval, with diffusivity β−1\beta^{-1} and initial susceptibility covariance at (θ,μ)(\theta,\mu). Write Zθ,u(t)=Zθu+tZ_{\theta,u}(t)=Z_\theta^{u+t} for 0≤t≤T0\le t\le T and Z∗=Zβ,0Z_*=Z_{\beta,0} for the stationary window. The two Gaussian laws may be realized on different probability spaces.

Theorem

Proposition S11.1 · Covariance and cubic-tensor relaxation

There are constants c>0c>0 and CC depending only on 𝖽,𝗌,a,β,γ,T,λ\mathsf d,\mathsf s,a,\beta,\gamma,T,\lambda such that, for every u≥0u\ge0 and every continuous bilinear form MM on 𝒳T\mathcal X_T of norm at most one,

∣𝔼M(Zγ,u,Zγ,u)−𝔼M(Z∗,Z∗)∣≤Ce−2κu.\big|\mathbb E M(Z_{\gamma,u},Z_{\gamma,u})-\mathbb E M(Z_*,Z_*)\big|\le C e^{-2\kappa u}.
(S11.1)

Let 𝒦γ,u\mathcal K_{\gamma,u} and 𝒦∗\mathcal K_* be any cubic path tensors satisfying the observation formula below for the preparations (γ,u)(\gamma,u) and (β,0)(\beta,0), respectively. In the projective tensor norm,

∥𝒦γ,u−𝒦∗∥π≤Ce−cu.\|\mathcal K_{\gamma,u}-\mathcal K_*\|_\pi\le C e^{-cu}.
(S11.2)

The covariance rate is explicit. The cubic estimate requires only some positive cc.

Corollary S11.2 · The stationary first correction

Take a particle realization of the quadratic gradient dynamics with initial law QN,γ(μ)Q_{N,\gamma}(\mu), the modulated Gibbs law at preparation γ\gamma. On the window [u,u+T][u,u+T], write ζN,u\zeta_{N,u} for the path of the empirical measure centered at its own expectation and multiplied by N\sqrt N. Fix cubic tensors 𝒦γ,u\mathcal K_{\gamma,u} for all u≥0u\ge0 and 𝒦∗\mathcal K_* for the stationary window, as in Proposition S11.1. Write A(W,K;Φ)A(W,K;\Phi) for the expected cubic pairing defined below.

For N≥2N\ge2, suppose εN\varepsilon_N bounds the moving-window error uniformly over u≥0u\ge0 and the unit ball of Cb5(𝒳T)C_b^5(\mathcal X_T):

∣N(𝔼Φ(ζN,u)−𝔼Φ(Zγ,u))−16A(Zγ,u,𝒦γ,u;Φ)∣≤εN.\left|\sqrt N\bigl(\mathbb E\Phi(\zeta_{N,u})-\mathbb E\Phi(Z_{\gamma,u})\bigr)-\tfrac16 A(Z_{\gamma,u},\mathcal K_{\gamma,u};\Phi)\right|\le\varepsilon_N.
(S11.3)

Define the stationary remainder by

RN,u(Φ)=𝔼Φ(ζN,u)−𝔼Φ(Z∗)−16NA(Z∗,𝒦∗;Φ).R_{N,u}(\Phi)=\mathbb E\Phi(\zeta_{N,u})-\mathbb E\Phi(Z_*)-\tfrac{1}{6\sqrt N} A(Z_*,\mathcal K_*;\Phi).
(S11.4)

There are constants c>0c>0 and CC, depending only on the parameters in Proposition S11.1, for which

sup⁡∥Φ∥Cb5≤1∣RN,u(Φ)∣≤εNN+Ce−2κu+CNe−cu.\sup_{\|\Phi\|_{C_b^5}\le1}|R_{N,u}(\Phi)|\le\frac{\varepsilon_N}{\sqrt N}+C e^{-2\kappa u}+\frac{C}{\sqrt N} e^{-cu}.
(S11.5)

For a sequence of such realizations, assume εN→0\varepsilon_N\to0. If uN≥0u_N\ge0 and Ne−2κuN→0\sqrt N e^{-2\kappa u_N}\to0, then sup⁡∥Φ∥Cb5≤1∣RN,uN(Φ)∣=o(N−1/2)\sup_{\|\Phi\|_{C_b^5}\le1}|R_{N,u_N}(\Phi)|=o(N^{-1/2}). In particular this holds if, for some δ>0\delta>0 and all N≥2N\ge2,

uN≥(14κ+δ)log⁡N.u_N\ge\left(\frac{1}{4\kappa}+\delta\right)\log N.
(S11.6)

The Poincaré constant

The measure μ\mu satisfies the Poincaré inequality with constant λ\lambda if every real C1C^1 function f∈L2(μ)f\in L^2(\mu) with bounded gradient satisfies

λ Var⁡μ(f)≤∫∣∇f∣2 dμ.\lambda\,\operatorname{Var}_\mu(f)\le\int |\nabla f|^2\,\mathrm d\mu.
(S11.7)

The proposition and corollary take λ>0\lambda>0.

Definition

The particle dynamics

The driving noise is a vector Brownian motion independent of the initial configuration. The particle paths give a strong solution on every finite time interval. There is a filtration under which a Brownian motion W′W' has coordinates Wi,j′=WijW'_{i,j}=W_i^j almost surely for every time and index. The particle paths are adapted and progressively measurable for this same filtration. Corollary S11.2 prescribes the initial law separately.

Definition

The cubic path tensor

For preparation θ\theta and shift uu, the cubic path tensor is specified by the three-observation formula of Theorem S2.1(iii) at the constant trajectory ρr=ρβ\rho^r=\rho_\beta. It is a symmetric element of 𝒳T⊗^π3\mathcal X_T^{\widehat\otimes_\pi3}. Choose three times τi∈[0,T]\tau_i\in[0,T], smooth oscillator tests ϕi∈Σ24𝖽+240\phi_i\in\Sigma^{24\mathsf d+240}, and put ti=u+τit_i=u+\tau_i. For each such choice there must exist cross-covariance densities χir\chi_i^r for all 0≤r≤u+T0\le r\le u+T. They satisfy χir/ρβ∈L2(μ)\chi_i^r/\rho_\beta\in L^2(\mu) and, for every oscillator test ψ\psi,

∫ψ χir dx=Cov⁡(Zθr(ψ),Zθti(ϕi)),\int\psi\,\chi_i^r\,\mathrm dx=\operatorname{Cov}(Z_\theta^r(\psi),Z_\theta^{t_i}(\phi_i)),
(S11.8)

with integrable product. For every such family of densities, the tensor evaluated on the trilinear form M(w1,w2,w3)=∏iwi(τi)(ϕi)M(w_1,w_2,w_3)=\prod_i w_i(\tau_i)(\phi_i) must equal the three terms of that formula at the physical times tit_i. All its spatial and time integrals must exist as specified there.
